Please perform a full structural rewrite of the white paper rather than incrementally editing the existing Sections 3–6.

We have materially simplified and corrected the conceptual framework after reviewing the original methodology and the regulatory treatment of securitisation SPVs. The revised paper should be rebuilt around the distinction between contractual collateral mechanics and actual funding economics.

1. Core conceptual change

Remove the current regulatory-recognition-based three-channel framework built around quantities such as

$$ C_t=\min(V_t,q_{\rm reg}B_t),\quad U_t,\quad A_t,\quad G_t,\quad X_t $$

and remove the associated derivations, identities, correction terms and general-\(r_{\rm alt}\) appendix material.

In particular, regulatory recognized collateral value \(q_{\rm reg}B_t\) should not be treated as an intermediate funding boundary. A regulatory haircut determines the value at which collateral is recognized for regulatory margin purposes; it does not determine the amount of cash that can be raised from that collateral.

The core economic chain should instead be:

$$ \boxed{\text{CSA / transaction terms}\rightarrow B_t} $$ $$ \boxed{\text{reuse + repo market}\rightarrow F_t} $$ $$ \boxed{\text{actual funding surplus/shortfall}\rightarrow ColVA/FVA}. $$
2. Contractual collateral amount

Let

$$ V_t>0 $$

denote positive swap MTM from the dealer's perspective.

Let \(q_{\rm CSA}=1-h_{\rm CSA}\) denote the contractual collateral valuation percentage under the CSA/transaction documentation.

In the uncapped case:

$$ B_t=\frac{V_t}{q_{\rm CSA}}. $$

For a SPIRE/repack structure with a finite collateral pool, allow:

$$ B_t= \min\left( \frac{V_t}{q_{\rm CSA}}, B_t^{\rm cap} \right), $$

subject to confirmation from the actual transaction documentation that this correctly represents the contractual mechanics when the pool is exhausted.

Do not assume that a binding pool cap automatically creates a regulatory VM deficiency or requires cash substitution. What happens when the cap binds is a contractual/legal question.

Clarify the role of the CSA collateral requirement. The CSA/transaction documentation—not the VA model—determines the amount and type of collateral delivered. The contractual valuation percentage \(q_{\rm CSA}\) may reflect negotiated credit-support/risk-management terms and does not require a regulatory haircut to justify its existence. For example, \(q_{\rm CSA}=90\%\) means €1 of eligible bond market value counts as €0.90 of contractual credit support, so absent a structural cap, \(V_t=100\) implies \(B_t=111.11\). The VA model should take this contractual collateral process as an input rather than independently determining how much collateral ought to be posted. If a pool cap binds, do not assume that the VA desk requires additional collateral or cash substitution; the residual treatment is determined by the transaction documentation.

3. Rehypothecation and repo funding

Let

$$ \rho_t\in\{0,1\} $$

represent whether received collateral is legally reusable/rehypothecatable.

Let

$$ q_{\rm repo}=1-h_{\rm repo} $$

be the actual repo advance rate / financing value of the bond.

Define maximum cash funding capacity from received collateral:

$$ F_t=\rho_t q_{\rm repo}B_t. $$

Make explicit that \(q_{\rm CSA}\) and \(q_{\rm repo}\) have completely different economic meanings:

$$ q_{\rm CSA}:\quad V_t\rightarrow\text{bond MV delivered}, $$ $$ q_{\rm repo}:\quad\text{bond MV}\rightarrow\text{cash funding capacity}. $$

Do not introduce \(q_{\rm reg}\) between these two steps.

4. Funding decomposition

Retain the methodology's assumption that positive swap MTM \(V_t\) is the relevant funding target, but state this explicitly as a base valuation/FVA methodology assumption, rather than deriving it from the existence of collateral.

Define:

$$ S_t=\min(V_t,F_t), $$ $$ U_t^f=(V_t-F_t)^+, $$ $$ X_t^f=(F_t-V_t)^+. $$

Here:

\(S_t\) is the portion of the trade's funding requirement that can be met through repo of received collateral;
\(U_t^f\) is the residual amount requiring unsecured funding;
\(X_t^f\) is genuine repo funding capacity beyond this trade's \(V_t\)-sized funding requirement.

Emphasize:

$$ V_t=S_t+U_t^f, \qquad F_t=S_t+X_t^f. $$

These should become the primary funding state variables, rather than appearing later as a “reduced-form representation.”

5. ColVA/FVA equation

Let \(r_{\rm base,t}\) denote the funding/discounting benchmark already embedded in base valuation, \(r_{\rm repo,t}\) the secured repo funding rate, and \(r_{\rm CoF,t}\) the dealer's marginal unsecured cost of funds.

Use the base-case assumption:

$$ r_{\rm alt,t}=r_{\rm CoF,t}, $$

justified by assuming the dealer/Treasury is a marginal net unsecured borrower, so genuine incremental repo cash can displace marginal unsecured funding elsewhere.

The main full-monetization formula should therefore be:

$$ \boxed{ ColVA_0= \int_0^T E_0\left[ DF_t^{\rm base} \left\{ S_t(r_{\rm repo,t}-r_{\rm base,t}) + U_t^f(r_{\rm CoF,t}-r_{\rm base,t}) + X_t^f(r_{\rm repo,t}-r_{\rm CoF,t}) \right\} \right]dt } $$

under the paper's sign convention that positive ColVA represents a cost and

$$ PV^{adj}=PV^{base}-ColVA. $$

Check the sign convention carefully and make it consistent throughout.

Also show the equivalent interpretation:

$$ V_t(r_{\rm CoF,t}-r_{\rm base,t}) + S_t(r_{\rm repo,t}-r_{\rm CoF,t}) + X_t^f(r_{\rm repo,t}-r_{\rm CoF,t}), $$

where applicable.

Explain economically: start from funding the \(V_t\)-sized position unsecured; repo funding replaces unsecured funding for \(S_t\); genuine additional repo capacity \(X_t^f\), if monetized, displaces marginal unsecured funding elsewhere.

6. Alternative funding policy

Retain a secondary policy in which the dealer repos received collateral only up to the funding requirement of this trade and does not monetize genuine excess:

$$ R_t=S_t=\min(V_t,F_t). $$

Then:

$$ \boxed{ ColVA_0^{\rm trade-only} = \int E_0[ DF_t^{\rm base} \{ S_t(r_{\rm repo,t}-r_{\rm base,t}) + U_t^f(r_{\rm CoF,t}-r_{\rm base,t}) \} ]dt } $$

or equivalently

$$ V_t(r_{\rm CoF,t}-r_{\rm base,t}) + S_t(r_{\rm repo,t}-r_{\rm CoF,t}). $$

State clearly that the two policies are identical whenever

$$ F_t\le V_t $$

and differ only when genuine excess repo capacity exists:

$$ F_t>V_t. $$
7. Regulatory margin treatment

Do not carry \(q_{\rm reg}\) as a core ColVA state variable.

Move regulatory margin treatment to the Regulatory and Contractual Basis section and explain its upstream role.

The paper should say that regulatory non-cash collateral haircuts can matter when:

the transaction is within the applicable bilateral-margin regime; and
the regulatory collateral requirement is binding relative to the contractual collateral requirement.

In that situation regulation may influence the amount/type of collateral that must be delivered and hence indirectly affect \(B_t\). Once actual \(B_t\) is established, however, regulatory recognized value should not be treated as a separate cash-funding quantity.

Preserve the useful regulatory research for Canada, UK, EU and US, but substantially shorten it and frame it as scope/constraint analysis, not a third valuation channel.

8. SPIRE / securitisation-SPV treatment

Add a concise but important discussion that securitisation SPVs can receive special regulatory treatment.

For the UK, discuss Article 30a of the UK bilateral-margin technical standards concerning qualifying OTC derivatives entered into by securitisation special-purpose entities. Explain accurately that, subject to the provision's conditions, the securitisation SPV is not required to post variation margin, while it may collect cash VM from its counterparty, and neither counterparty is required to post or collect initial margin.

Do not state that “SPIRE is exempt” unless the transaction-specific conditions have been verified. Instead state:

The applicability of the securitisation-SPV treatment is a transaction-specific legal determination. If applicable, a binding collateral-pool cap does not by itself imply failure to satisfy a regulatory VM-posting requirement, because the SPV may not be subject to such a posting requirement.

Also briefly note that other jurisdictions can contain exclusions/exemptions for certain SPVs or intragroup transactions. Keep this subordinate to the core model.

9. Initial Margin / mysterious HC(IM)

Do not infer the meaning of HC(IM)=96% in the methodology correspondence.

Explicitly flag this as an open methodology question:

Does IM mean Initial Margin?
Is 96% a regulatory collateral valuation percentage?
Is it an internal collateral/funding haircut?
Or does it represent the effective secured-financing/repo advance rate?

The paper must not equate a regulatory/IM haircut with repo funding proceeds without evidence.

Also distinguish actual Initial Margin from VM. If regulatory IM is relevant, its funding economics/MVA are conceptually separate from the non-cash VM ColVA model. Do not add an MVA model unless needed for scope.

10. Reuse and legal rights

Retain the discussion of rehypothecation/reuse rights, because this remains fundamental:

$$ \rho=0\Rightarrow F_t=0. $$

A bond may have collateral value while providing no funding benefit if the dealer cannot legally reuse it.

Make clear that regulatory recognition, contractual eligibility and legal reuse are separate questions.

11. Remove obsolete material

Delete, rather than merely de-emphasize, all material whose sole purpose was supporting the previous \(q_{\rm reg}\)-based three-channel model, including:

$$ C_t=\min(V_t,q_{\rm reg}B_t), $$

\(A_t,G_t,U_t,X_t\), the old Channel 1/2/3 decomposition, the distinction between old \(X_t=(F_t-C_t)^+\) and genuine excess, the bridge variable \((S_t-C_t)^+\), the general-\(r_{\rm alt}\) correction term, and proofs showing equivalence between that framework and the reduced-form model.

These were useful diagnostically but are no longer part of the desired model.

In particular, remove the current appendix formula containing

$$ (S_t-C_t)^+(r_{\rm CoF,t}-r_{\rm alt,t}), $$

since that correction exists only because the previous framework used regulatory recognition as an artificial funding boundary.
