Please draft a new technical white paper on:

    CVA for Derivatives Collateralized by Non-Cash Variation Margin

Use the existing “FVA / ColVA for Non-Cash VM” paper as the reference for transaction structure, notation, CSA collateral mechanics, SPIRE context, legal/regulatory background, and writing style.

However, DO NOT mechanically adapt the FVA equations into CVA equations.

The purpose of this paper is to derive the CVA treatment independently from first principles. The fundamental economic question is different:

    FVA/ColVA:
        How much funding can the dealer obtain from received collateral,
        and at what funding cost?

    CVA:
        If the counterparty defaults, what loss does the dealer suffer
        after taking account of the collateral that can actually be
        realized through the close-out/liquidation process?

The new paper should be concise, rigorous, self-contained, and structured so that the economic logic is established before implementation details or regulatory background.

============================================================
1. SCOPE AND CENTRAL QUESTION
============================================================

Begin with a short Scope section.

Consider the same asset-side case as the FVA paper:

    V_t > 0

where the client/counterparty owes the dealer and posts non-cash collateral
(e.g. bonds) as variation margin.

The paper should answer:

    How should non-cash collateral modify the dealer’s CVA exposure and
    loss at counterparty default?

State explicitly that the liability-side case is outside scope unless needed
for symmetry.

Also distinguish clearly between:

1. pricing/accounting CVA;
2. regulatory CVA capital / counterparty credit-risk capital.

The core paper should address economic/pricing CVA. Regulatory capital
treatment may be discussed separately in an appendix and must not silently
determine the pricing-CVA formula.

============================================================
2. START FROM UNCOLLATERALIZED CVA
============================================================

Establish the ordinary CVA framework first.

For counterparty default time tau, write the generic loss-at-default concept as

    Loss_tau
        = LGD × positive residual exposure at default

and CVA schematically as

    CVA_0
        = E_0[
            DF(0,tau)
            × loss at tau
            × 1_{tau <= T}
          ]

or the equivalent survival/default-intensity representation used by the
firm’s existing CVA framework.

Do not unnecessarily rebuild the firm’s entire CVA infrastructure.

The purpose is to identify how non-cash collateral changes the exposure/loss
term supplied to the existing CVA machinery.

Make clear that the economically relevant object is exposure at/after default,
not the ordinary pre-default funding capacity of collateral.

============================================================
3. IMPORT THE COLLATERAL PROCESS FROM THE FVA PAPER
============================================================

Reuse the FVA paper’s contractual collateral process.

Let:

    V_t       = derivative exposure / cash-equivalent VM requirement
                from dealer perspective

    q_CSA     = CSA contractual valuation percentage = 1 - h_CSA

    B_t       = market value of bond collateral delivered

For an uncapped collateral process:

    B_t = V_t / q_CSA

For the SPIRE / finite-pool case, subject to confirmation of transaction
documentation:

    B_t = min(V_t / q_CSA, B_t^cap)

Use the same caveat as the FVA paper:

The CSA / transaction documentation determines how much collateral is
actually delivered. The CVA model does not independently determine what the
counterparty “should” post.

If the collateral pool cap binds, do NOT automatically assume cash
substitution, additional collateral, regulatory breach, or termination.
Those are contractual/legal questions.

Import B_t as the collateral process into the CVA model.

============================================================
4. EXPLICITLY SEPARATE THE TWO HAIRCUT QUESTIONS
============================================================

This distinction is central to the paper.

The FVA paper contains:

    q_CSA
        contractual valuation percentage determining bond quantity B_t

and

    q_repo
        repo advance rate determining cash funding capacity

        F_t = rho q_repo B_t.

For CVA, do NOT automatically use q_repo.

Repo haircut answers:

    “How much cash can the dealer raise against the bond in the repo market?”

CVA instead asks:

    “What economic value of the collateral is available to reduce the
     dealer’s loss following counterparty default?”

These are different questions.

Introduce a separate object if required, for example

    L_tau

or

    C_tau^real

for the collateral value actually realizable in the default/close-out process.

If a reduced-form haircut is eventually used, give it a distinct name such as

    q_liq = 1 - h_liq

only after explaining exactly what risk it represents.

Do not identify

    q_liq = q_repo
or
    q_liq = q_reg
or
    q_liq = q_CSA

without an explicit methodological justification.

============================================================
5. DEFAULT AND CLOSE-OUT TIMELINE
============================================================

This should be one of the central sections.

Lay out the event sequence naturally:

    last successful margin exchange
        ->
    counterparty default tau
        ->
    margin period of risk / close-out period
        ->
    derivative close-out value determined
        ->
    collateral available/enforceable
        ->
    collateral liquidated or otherwise realized
        ->
    residual unsecured claim / surplus collateral returned

Introduce notation only as needed, for example:

    tau       = counterparty default time
    delta     = effective MPOR / liquidation horizon
    V_tau+δ   = derivative close-out exposure
    B_tau     = collateral held when margining stops
    P^B_tau+δ = collateral value at realization
    C^real_tau = realizable collateral value

The exact timing convention should follow the firm’s existing CVA methodology
where available.

The paper must explicitly investigate whether collateral should be frozen at
the last margin call before default while the derivative and collateral values
continue evolving over MPOR.

Do not simply evaluate

    V_tau - q B_tau

at the same instant unless that approximation is justified.

============================================================
6. THE CORE CVA EXPOSURE EQUATION
============================================================

Build the loss equation from the close-out mechanics.

A natural generic structure to investigate is

    E_tau^net
        = (CloseOut_tau - C_tau^real)^+

and

    Loss_tau
        = LGD × E_tau^net,

subject to the precise legal close-out and recovery convention.

If the model explicitly simulates the collateral value over MPOR, the
realizable collateral may look schematically like

    C_tau^real
        = enforceable value of B_tau at tau + delta
          net of liquidation effects/costs.

If a reduced-form liquidation haircut is used instead:

    C_tau^real ≈ q_liq B_tau

or the appropriate time-shifted equivalent.

Do NOT assume this formula is final without checking the existing firm CVA
framework.

The paper should identify exactly where non-cash collateral enters the
existing CVA exposure calculation.

============================================================
7. MPOR IS POTENTIALLY THE KEY NON-CASH COLLATERAL EFFECT
============================================================

Explain why a bond worth B at the last successful margin call is not
necessarily worth B when it is needed after default.

During MPOR:

1. derivative MTM can move;
2. bond collateral price can move;
3. FX can move if collateral and exposure currencies differ;
4. liquidation may take time;
5. bid/offer and liquidation costs may arise.

Therefore the CVA problem potentially requires the JOINT evolution of

    derivative exposure
and
    collateral value.

If both are stochastic, emphasize:

    E[(V - C)^+] != (E[V] - E[C])^+

in general.

Therefore expected exposure profiles and expected collateral profiles should
not normally be subtracted independently before applying the positive part.

Pathwise evaluation may be required.

============================================================
8. WRONG-WAY / RIGHT-WAY RISK
============================================================

Treat this explicitly.

There are potentially two distinct dependence structures:

A. exposure-collateral dependence

    correlation between derivative MTM and bond collateral value;

B. default-market dependence

    dependence between counterparty default and either derivative exposure
    or collateral value.

For example, CVA can be materially worse if counterparty default tends to
occur precisely when:

    derivative exposure rises
and/or
    collateral value falls.

Do not call every correlation “wrong-way risk.”

Use precise terminology:

    exposure/collateral correlation

versus

    default dependence / wrong-way risk.

If the current CVA engine assumes independence, state that clearly as a
modeling assumption and identify the omitted effect.

============================================================
9. CSA HAIRCUT: WHAT DOES IT DO FOR CVA?
============================================================

Analyze carefully the economic role of q_CSA.

Example:

    V = 100
    q_CSA = 0.90
    B = 111.11.

At the margining instant:

    q_CSA B = 100,

but the dealer physically holds bonds with current market value 111.11.

Do NOT automatically conclude either:

    “CVA collateral value is 100”

or

    “CVA collateral value is 111.11.”

Explain what the CSA haircut actually accomplishes:

It creates an overcollateralization / valuation buffer intended to protect
against collateral-value and close-out risks.

The CVA model must determine how that buffer interacts with:

    MPOR,
    collateral price evolution,
    close-out exposure,
    liquidation value,
    return of excess collateral,
    recovery.

This is an important conceptual distinction from the FVA model.

============================================================
10. RECOVERY AND COLLATERAL MUST NOT BE DOUBLE COUNTED
============================================================

Explicitly derive the ordering.

The paper should establish whether the firm's convention is conceptually:

    first collateralize/net the exposure,
    then apply recovery/LGD to the remaining unsecured claim,

e.g.

    Loss_tau
        = (1 - R)
          (CloseOut_tau - C_tau^real)^+

or something different.

Do not apply LGD independently to gross exposure and then separately credit
collateral unless mathematically justified.

Check treatment of:

    collateral surplus,
    undercollateralization,
    limited recourse,
    close-out netting,
    recovery of residual unsecured claims.

============================================================
11. REHYPOTHECATION: DO NOT IMPORT THE FVA ROLE BLINDLY
============================================================

In the FVA paper, rho is central because:

    rho = 0 -> no repo funding capacity.

For CVA, investigate separately whether rehypothecation changes loss-at-default
economics.

The mere fact that collateral cannot be repoed before default does not
necessarily mean it provides zero credit protection.

Conversely, if collateral has already been rehypothecated when the
counterparty defaults, determine how the dealer’s obligation to return
equivalent collateral and the linked repo obligation affect close-out loss.

Do not assume:

    rho = 0 -> collateral value for CVA = 0.

Funding accessibility and credit-risk mitigation are separate concepts.

This distinction should be highlighted explicitly because it is one of the
most important differences between the FVA and CVA papers.

============================================================
12. SPIRE / LIMITED-RECOURSE STRUCTURE
============================================================

Use the FVA paper’s SPIRE background but ask a different question.

For CVA, the key issue is not merely whether B_t^cap limits VM delivery.

Investigate what the dealer can recover when SPIRE defaults given:

    compartment / series segregation,
    limited recourse,
    collateral pool,
    priority of payments,
    swap-counterparty ranking,
    close-out provisions,
    security interests / title transfer,
    claims on residual collateral.

If the swap counterparty has direct or senior access to the collateral pool,
ordinary unsecured-counterparty LGD assumptions may be inappropriate.

Do NOT infer the recovery model from the fact that SPIRE is an SPV.

Flag this for legal / credit methodology confirmation.

============================================================
13. REGULATORY HAIRCUTS
============================================================

Keep this OUT of the core CVA formula unless methodology requires it.

As established in the FVA paper, q_reg answers a regulatory collateral
recognition / adequacy question.

It is not automatically:

    actual liquidation value,
    repo advance rate,
    CSA valuation percentage,
    CVA loss haircut.

If regulatory CVA / counterparty-credit-risk capital uses prescribed
collateral haircuts, explain that separately in a regulatory appendix.

Do not allow regulatory capital methodology to leak silently into
pricing/accounting CVA.

============================================================
14. WORKED EXAMPLES
============================================================

After deriving the framework, include a small number of intuitive examples.

At minimum:

Case 1 — perfect collateral / no MPOR movement
    V = 100
    q_CSA = 0.90
    B = 111.11
    show what happens if collateral remains sufficient at close-out.

Case 2 — collateral price falls during MPOR
    demonstrate residual exposure despite apparently sufficient collateral
    before default.

Case 3 — derivative exposure rises during MPOR
    demonstrate gap exposure.

Case 4 — pool cap binds
    B^cap < V/q_CSA
    demonstrate pre-existing undercollateralization plus MPOR risk.

Optionally:

Case 5 — favorable collateral movement / collateral surplus
    explain why CVA cannot become a windfall simply because collateral value
    exceeds the close-out claim; excess collateral generally creates a return
    obligation subject to legal terms.

Keep examples economic and transparent rather than numerically elaborate.

Include a short comparison section or summary table.

The central contrast should be something like:

    Question             FVA / ColVA                CVA
    ---------------------------------------------------------------
    Main object          funding cost               default loss
    Contract input       B_t                        B_t
    Market conversion    repo funding capacity      realizable collateral

============================================================
16. IMPLEMENTATION / SIMULATION
============================================================

Only after the economics are established, describe implementation.

Identify what can be reused from the existing CVA simulation framework:

    derivative exposure paths,
    default probabilities/intensities,
    discount factors,
    recovery assumptions,
    MPOR framework,
    netting sets.

Then identify what non-cash collateral adds:

    collateral quantity process B_t,
    bond price evolution,
    CSA haircut/top-up process,
    pool cap,
    collateral/exposure correlation,
    FX where applicable,
    liquidation treatment.

If joint pathwise simulation is required, state that explicitly.

Do not prescribe a new stochastic model unless necessary; distinguish
methodology requirements from implementation choices.

============================================================
17. OPEN QUESTIONS / REQUIRED CONFIRMATIONS
============================================================

End the paper with a prominent list of unresolved questions rather than
burying assumptions in the derivation.

At minimum investigate/confirm:

1. What exact exposure/close-out quantity does the existing CVA engine use at
   default and over MPOR?

2. How does the existing CVA methodology treat cash VM?

3. For non-cash VM, what collateral value is recognized for pricing CVA:
   current MV, stressed/liquidation MV, supervisory haircut value, or another
   quantity?

4. Is there an explicit liquidation haircut? If yes, how is it calibrated and
   is it distinct from q_CSA, q_repo and q_reg?

5. How is MPOR implemented, and at what point is the collateral quantity
   frozen?

6. Is collateral price simulated pathwise during MPOR?

7. Is correlation between derivative exposure and collateral value modeled?

8. Is default dependence / wrong-way risk modeled?

9. How is recovery applied after collateral/netting?

10. What happens contractually when the SPIRE collateral pool cap binds?

11. What rights does the swap counterparty have against the SPIRE collateral
    pool upon default, and where does it rank in the waterfall?

12. Does limited recourse alter the appropriate LGD/recovery treatment?

13. Does rehypothecation affect the CVA close-out mechanics, or only FVA?

14. Does the existing methodology already contain a non-cash collateral CVA
    treatment that should be reused rather than reconstructed?

15. What exactly was HC(IM)=96% in the original methodology, and does it have
    any legitimate role in CVA? Do not assume that it does.

============================================================
18. WRITING / STRUCTURAL REQUIREMENTS
============================================================

Keep the main paper short and logically ordered.

Recommended structure:

    Scope

    1. Setup and notation
       1.1 CVA loss convention
       1.2 Contractual collateral process
       1.3 The distinct collateral valuation concepts

    2. Default and close-out mechanics
       2.1 Default timeline and MPOR
       2.2 Realizable collateral value
       2.3 Residual exposure and recovery

    3. CVA methodology
       3.1 Pathwise net exposure
       3.2 CVA equation
       3.3 Non-cash collateral effects
       3.4 Correlation and wrong-way risk

    4. Worked examples

    5. Relationship to FVA / ColVA

    6. Summary

    Appendix A — Modeling and implementation choices
    Appendix B — SPIRE contractual / legal considerations
    Appendix C — Regulatory treatment
    Appendix D — Open questions and required confirmations
    Appendix E — References

Follow these writing principles:

- Derive economics before notation proliferation.
- Every variable must answer a concrete economic question.
- Do not create multiple “channels” unless genuinely necessary.
- Prefer a small number of state variables and identities.
- Clearly distinguish FACT, ASSUMPTION, and OPEN QUESTION.
- Do not present transaction-specific legal conclusions as established facts
  unless supported by documentation.
- Do not state that SPIRE receives an exemption merely because it is an SPV.
- Do not reuse FVA concepts simply because they already exist.
- Keep regulatory material out of the main pricing derivation unless it
  actually changes the economic CVA calculation.
- Use the existing FVA paper as a source for collateral mechanics and
  terminology, but treat the CVA derivation as a separate first-principles
  exercise.

The conceptual chain of the new paper should ultimately be visually obvious:

    CSA / transaction terms
             |
             v
       collateral B_t
             |
             v
    counterparty default
             |
             v
      MPOR / close-out
        /          \
 derivative       collateral
 exposure         value
        \          /
             v
      realizable net exposure
             |
             v
       recovery / LGD
             |
             v
             CVA

The reader should finish the paper understanding not merely the formula, but
WHY q_CSA, q_repo, q_reg, and any CVA liquidation haircut answer four
different questions and therefore must not be conflated.
