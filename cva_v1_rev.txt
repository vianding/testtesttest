Please revise the current first draft of:

    “CVA for Non-Cash Variation Margin”

This is a TARGETED METHODOLOGY REVIEW AND REVISION, not a rewrite from scratch.

The current draft is conceptually strong. Preserve its main architecture, equations, default/MPOR timeline, pathwise treatment, relationship to the FVA/ColVA companion note, code-inspection findings, and FACT / ASSUMPTION / OPEN discipline.

The objective of this revision is to:

1. tighten several conceptual statements;
2. avoid giving weakly supported quantities undue prominence;
3. distinguish fundamental economics from optional reduced-form parameterizations;
4. soften transaction-specific claims that require legal/credit confirmation;
5. sharpen the implementation implications of the existing CVA engine;
6. make the unresolved questions more decision-oriented and prioritized.

Do NOT add theory merely to make the paper longer.

================================================================
1. MAKE C^real FUNDAMENTAL; DEMOTE q_liq TO A PARAMETERIZATION
================================================================

The current draft gives q_liq substantial prominence.

Please change the conceptual hierarchy.

The fundamental CVA object is:

    C^real_{tau+delta}
        = collateral value actually enforceable and realizable
          through the close-out / liquidation process.

The CVA loss is fundamentally:

    E^net_{tau+delta}
        = (V_{tau+delta} - C^real_{tau+delta})^+

    Loss_{tau+delta}
        = (1-R) E^net_{tau+delta}.

Only AFTER defining C^real should a reduced-form representation such as

    C^real_{tau+delta}
        = q_liq Pi_{tau+delta}

be introduced.

Make explicit:

    C^real is economically fundamental.
    q_liq is an optional reduced-form implementation parameter.

This matters because if the collateral security itself is simulated pathwise
through MPOR, much of the relevant collateral risk is already captured in

    Pi_{tau+delta}
      = B_tlast * p_{tau+delta}/p_tlast.

For a liquid sovereign bond, q_liq may therefore be close to 1 and may capture
only residual execution effects such as:

    bid/offer,
    market impact,
    operational/legal liquidation costs.

Do NOT assume a material q_liq haircut is required.

An entirely legitimate production conclusion may be:

    q_liq = 1

unless evidence supports a separate liquidation adjustment.

Accordingly, avoid statements such as:

    “The CVA collateral value is q_liq B”

without the MPOR revaluation.

The more precise expression is:

    C^real_{tau+delta}
      = q_liq B_tlast p_{tau+delta}/p_tlast

under the reduced-form representation.

================================================================
2. KEEP q_reg OUT OF THE CORE MODEL AND VISUALLY DEMOTE IT
================================================================

The companion FVA analysis established that regulatory collateral recognition
is not a core pricing variable here.

In particular, if the particular SPIRE transaction qualifies for the
securitisation-SPV treatment under UK Article 30a, the SPV does not have the
ordinary regulatory VM-posting requirement.

Do NOT state that all securitisation SPVs are exempt; applicability remains a
transaction-specific legal determination.

However, q_reg should NOT visually appear to be a peer production input to:

    q_CSA,
    q_repo,
    C^real / q_liq.

Please revise §1.3.

Instead of:

    “Four collateral valuation concepts”

prefer:

    “Distinct collateral valuation concepts”

The three quantities relevant to the two pricing notes are:

    q_CSA
        contractual valuation percentage determining collateral quantity;

    q_repo
        repo advance rate relevant to FVA/ColVA only;

    C^real / optionally q_liq
        post-default realizable collateral relevant to CVA.

Then mention q_reg separately, perhaps in a short paragraph or footnote:

    Regulatory collateral valuation percentages can apply under bilateral
    margin / regulatory capital frameworks. They are not pricing-CVA inputs.
    If the SPIRE transaction qualifies for the securitisation-SPV treatment
    discussed in Appendix C, q_reg also does not determine SPIRE's VM posting
    amount. Regulatory treatment is therefore confined to Appendix C.

Preserve Appendix C for completeness, but do not allow q_reg to look like a
fourth state variable in the pricing methodology.

================================================================
3. EMPHASIZE THE REAL NON-CASH EFFECT: QUANTITY FREEZES, VALUE DOES NOT
================================================================

This is one of the strongest results in the draft and should become even more
central.

At the last successful margin exchange t_last:

    B_tlast = N p_tlast.

After margining stops:

    N is frozen.

But at close-out:

    Pi_{tau+delta}
       = N p_{tau+delta}
       = B_tlast p_{tau+delta}/p_tlast.

Therefore the key non-cash VM effect is:

    frozen security quantity
        +
    stochastic collateral price through MPOR.

Contrast explicitly with cash VM:

    cash amount frozen -> nominal collateral amount remains essentially fixed
    over MPOR (apart from remuneration/accrual);

    bond quantity frozen -> collateral market value continues to evolve.

Retain the statement:

    “quantity freezes; value does not”

because it is an excellent summary.

But make sure the paper does not imply that the price ratio itself is the only
possible non-cash effect if FX, indexation, substitution or other mechanics
also matter.

================================================================
4. SHARPEN THE IMPLEMENTATION FINDING ABOUT THE CURRENT MPOR LAG
================================================================

Appendix A.5 may contain the most important practical finding in the paper.

The existing engine appears to evaluate something resembling:

    (V_t - kappa A_{t-delta})^+

because the collateral cube is lagged by the MPOR.

That is appropriate for cash VM because the posted cash amount genuinely
freezes at its last-call amount.

For bond collateral, however, the economically desired quantity is closer to:

    (V_t - kappa N_{t-delta} p_t)^+

or equivalently:

    [V_t
     - kappa B_{t-delta} p_t/p_{t-delta}]^+.

This freezes the SECURITY QUANTITY at the last successful margin date while
revaluing that quantity at the close-out date.

Please make this distinction extremely explicit.

Do not state conclusively that the production engine is “wrong” until the
code-reading interpretation has been validated numerically.

Label the current finding appropriately as:

    code-reading inference / implementation hypothesis.

Then propose the highest-value validation experiment:

Run otherwise identical exposure calculations with:

    1. existing collateral lag ON;
    2. collateral lag OFF / collateral valued on exposure date;
    3. where possible, explicitly frozen quantity revalued at exposure date.

Compare the resulting pathwise E^net / CVA profiles.

The experiment should establish whether the current lag suppresses the
non-cash collateral MPOR effect and quantify its magnitude.

================================================================
5. PRESERVE AND EMPHASIZE EQUATION (17)
================================================================

Equation (17) is one of the strongest explanatory equations in the paper.

Retain the decomposition:

    V_{tau+delta} - C^real_{tau+delta}

into:

    (i) structural position at the last successful margin exchange;

    (ii) derivative MPOR move;

    (iii) collateral MPOR move.

Use it as the organizing explanation of where CVA comes from.

Make clear that:

    (i) captures the starting collateralization buffer/shortfall;

    (ii) exists for cash and non-cash VM;

    (iii) is the distinctive bond/non-cash VM channel.

If q_liq is retained, explain that the structural term depends on q_liq/q_CSA
under that reduced-form parameterization.

Do not let this algebra imply that q_liq itself is necessarily material.

================================================================
6. PRESERVE THE CSA HAIRCUT INSIGHT, BUT WORD IT PRECISELY
================================================================

The V=100, q_CSA=0.90, B=111.11 example is excellent.

Keep it.

The key result is NOT that CVA should use either:

    100
or
    111.11.

Instead:

    q_CSA determines how much bond is posted.

That creates an economic overcollateralization buffer in raw bond MV.

Whether that buffer survives through default/MPOR depends on:

    collateral price evolution,
    derivative exposure evolution,
    liquidation effects,
    pool-cap mechanics.

Under the q_liq representation, the initial effective protection is governed
by q_liq/q_CSA.

But again, q_CSA sets QUANTITY; it is not itself a CVA collateral haircut.

================================================================
7. SOFTEN THE SPIRE DEFAULT / COLLATERAL IMPAIRMENT CLAIMS
================================================================

§3.4.1 contains an important hypothesis, but some wording is currently too
strong.

Do not state as established fact that:

    SPIRE default and collapse in collateral price are effectively the same
    event.

Instead say something like:

    “The SPIRE structure may create materially stronger dependence between
    SPV default and collateral impairment than would arise for an unrelated
    bilateral counterparty.”

Explain why this could occur:

    the SPV's assets may substantially consist of the collateral pool;
    deterioration of that pool may affect the SPV's ability to perform.

But also recognize other possible default states:

    swap/transaction mechanics,
    operational/default events,
    liabilities or waterfall mechanics,
    events not associated with default of the collateral issuer.

Therefore distinguish clearly:

    collateral issuer default

from

    SPIRE / swap counterparty default.

Do not substitute collateral-issuer recovery for counterparty recovery.

The correct treatment of dependence is an OPEN methodology question requiring
legal/credit/model confirmation.

================================================================
8. DISTINGUISH THREE DIFFERENT “RECOVERY / HAIRCUT” OBJECTS
================================================================

Preserve and strengthen Appendix B.2.

The paper should make it almost impossible for a reader to conflate:

    R
        recovery on the counterparty's residual unsecured claim;

    R_coll
        recovery of the collateral issuer if the collateral security itself
        defaults;

    q_liq
        realization percentage for a performing collateral security sold
        during close-out.

These are fundamentally different random events / economic quantities.

A 65–75% sovereign recovery assumption should NOT automatically become:

    q_liq = 0.65–0.75

on every path.

If collateral issuer default and counterparty default are dependent, that
belongs in the JOINT STATE MODEL:

    default state
        ->
    collateral impairment/recovery state.

It should not normally be represented by applying the collateral issuer's
default recovery to the collateral on every counterparty-default path.

This distinction is potentially one of the most important findings of the
paper.

9. KEEP RECOVERY ORDERING CLEAN
================================================================

Preserve:

    Loss = (1-R)(V-C^real)^+.

Continue to emphasize:

    collateralize/net first;
    apply LGD once to the residual unsecured claim.

Do not allow:

    collateral haircut,
    collateral-issuer recovery,
    counterparty recovery

to be multiplied together without a clearly defined economic event sequence.

Keep the current warning about possible payoff-recovery vs
credit-curve-bootstrap-recovery inconsistency as OPEN rather than resolving it
without evidence.

10. REHYPOTHECATION SECTION: KEEP THE RESULT, SOFTEN THE ABSOLUTE CLAIM
================================================================

The distinction:

    funding accessibility != credit-risk mitigation

is excellent and should remain.

Preserve:

    rho = 0 does NOT imply C^real = 0.

However, avoid making an absolute statement that rehypothecation is always
irrelevant to CVA.

Preferred wording:

    “To first order, rehypothecation does not determine the collateral's
    credit-risk mitigation value, unlike its dominant role in FVA.”

If collateral has been repoed/reused, legal obligations to source and return
equivalent securities, repo-counterparty exposure, close-out interaction or
operational frictions may create secondary effects.

These should not enter the core CVA formula unless legal analysis shows they
are material.

================================================================
11. KEEP THE PATHWISE / JENSEN RESULT
================================================================

Retain:

    E[(V-C)^+] >= (E[V]-E[C])^+

and the conclusion that expected exposure and expected collateral profiles
must not simply be netted before flooring.

Joint pathwise simulation is the correct architecture if both V and
collateral value are stochastic.

However, distinguish carefully:

    exposure/collateral market-factor correlation

from

    counterparty-default dependence / wrong-way risk.

The first can be captured automatically if both cubes are generated on common
market paths.

The second requires dependence between tau and the market state and is NOT
automatically captured merely because V and collateral are simulated jointly.

================================================================
12. RECONCILE THE REPORTED-NUMBER / RECOVERY GROSS-UP SECTION
================================================================

Retain Appendix A.6 because the numerical observation is striking:

    828,314 / 248,494 ~= 1/(1-0.70).

But keep it explicitly as an observed arithmetic relationship, not a
conclusion about what the implementation intends.

Clarify the three possible quantities:

    CVA;
    expected loss / CVA before some reporting transformation;
    CDS/protection hedge notional.

If a protection notional is being reported, a gross-up by LGD may be
economically legitimate.

If a CVA is being reported, undoing an LGD multiplication is not.

And if a gross-up is required, the denominator should relate to the
counterparty recovery R, not automatically to the collateral scalar kappa.

This should remain a high-priority reconciliation item.

13. REORGANIZE THE OPEN QUESTIONS INTO FOUR DECISION BLOCKS
================================================================

The current 15-question list is useful but somewhat flat.

Keep the detailed questions if useful, but precede them with four prioritized
decision blocks.

PRIORITY 1 — Meaning of the collateral scalar kappa

    What does the current 0.70 scalar actually represent?

    Is it:
        collateral-issuer recovery?
        liquidation haircut?
        regulatory haircut?
        CSA valuation percentage?
        stress scalar?
        something else?

    This is likely the highest-impact question because it directly changes
    every path's collateral value.

PRIORITY 2 — MPOR / collateral lag mechanics

    Does the current implementation freeze collateral VALUE or security
    QUANTITY?

    Can the frozen quantity be revalued on the exposure/close-out date?

    Validate by numerical on/off experiment.

PRIORITY 3 — Default / legal / recovery structure

    What claim does the dealer actually have against SPIRE on default?

    Where does it rank?

    How does limited recourse affect residual recovery?

    How dependent are SPIRE default and collateral impairment?

    Which recovery R should apply to the residual unsecured claim?

PRIORITY 4 — Reported-number interpretation

    Is 248,494 CVA?
    Is 828,314 hedge notional?
    Why exactly does the 1/(1-0.70) transformation occur?

After these four blocks, retain lower-priority questions as secondary
confirmations.

14. DO NOT EXPAND THE THEORY FURTHER
================================================================

The paper already has enough theoretical machinery.

The next revision should become SHORTER or approximately the same length, not
longer.

Remove repetition where the same distinction appears in:

    main text,
    summary,
    appendix,
    open-question section.

Use cross-references instead.

The strongest production-methodology story should emerge clearly:

    CSA determines collateral quantity
              |
              v
    last successful margin exchange
              |
              v
    freeze security quantity
              |
              v
    counterparty default / MPOR
              |
        ----------------
        |              |
        v              v
    derivative      revalue frozen
    close-out       collateral quantity
        |              |
        ------    -------
              \  /
               v
        realizable residual exposure
               |
               v
        LGD on residual claim
               |
               v
              CVA

The paper should leave the reader with the possibility that the correct
non-cash CVA enhancement is surprisingly simple:

    keep the existing CVA machinery,
    keep pathwise collateral netting,
    but represent bond VM as a frozen SECURITY QUANTITY whose VALUE continues
    to evolve through MPOR.

A separate liquidation haircut q_liq should be introduced only if there is
methodological/economic evidence that execution costs are material.

================================================================
15. FINAL REVIEW STANDARD
================================================================

Before finalizing the revision, challenge every important statement using:

    Is this:
        FACT from code/documentation?
        an ASSUMPTION?
        an OPEN question?
        or a derived mathematical RESULT?

Do not upgrade an inference to a fact.

In particular, do not assume:

    q_reg applies to SPIRE;
    q_liq must differ materially from 1;
    HC(IM)=96% is relevant to CVA;
    kappa=0.70 is a liquidation haircut;
    collateral-issuer recovery equals counterparty recovery;
    SPIRE default implies collateral issuer default;
    rehypothecation determines CVA protection;
    the existing collateral lag has definitely been implemented incorrectly.

The revision should make the few remaining empirical/legal/methodology
questions easier to answer, rather than hiding them behind additional model
complexity.
