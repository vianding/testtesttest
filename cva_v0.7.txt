Please revise the latest version of the “Pricing CVA for Non-Cash VM / Sovereign Bond Collateral” note.

The current draft contains most of the required technical content and several important improvements, especially around collateral quantity, the finite collateral pool, pre-default deterioration, recovery modeling, MPOR, and implementation.

The next revision should NOT add more machinery. Its main objective is to simplify and strengthen the conceptual structure.

The document should read top-down from the CVA problem into the specific modeling components, with a clear distinction among:

- core economic/modeling principles;
- modeling/calibration choices;
- transaction-specific documentary assumptions;
- implementation refinements and sensitivities.

Do not mechanically patch individual paragraphs. Reorganize where necessary so the document reads as one CVA model being progressively decomposed.

======================================================================
1. RESTORE A TOP-DOWN, CVA-CENTERED STRUCTURE
======================================================================

Start from the economic object ultimately being calculated:

    CVA
      ->
    event exposure E_tau
      ->
    E_tau = (V_tau^uw - B_tau^uw)^+.

Define the two central event-state quantities early:

    V_tau^uw
        derivative value/claim determined in the contractual unwind state;

    B_tau^uw
        value of the posted bond collateral available to offset that claim
        in the contractual unwind state.

The central question of the paper is therefore:

    At the loss-trigger/unwind event, what is the derivative claim and
    what value of the posted sovereign bond is available against it?

Establish early that:

1. CVA remains expected loss on the derivative counterparty exposure.

2. The special feature of this transaction is that, under the assumed
   transaction mechanics, default/restructuring of the third-party sovereign
   collateral issuer both:
       (a) causes termination/unwind of the derivative; and
       (b) changes/immediately impairs the value of the collateral supporting
           the resulting derivative claim.

3. Therefore the relevant exposure is not the performing-state collateralized
   exposure immediately before the event. It is:

       E_tau = (V_tau^uw - B_tau^uw)^+.

After establishing this overall CVA/exposure framework, decompose the problem
into three questions:

    (i)   How much bond principal/notional is actually posted immediately
          before the event?

    (ii)  What is that posted bond worth for purposes of the unwind once the
          default/restructuring event has occurred?

    (iii) What is the derivative unwind claim in that same state?

The subsequent sections should answer those questions.

Avoid making the reader work through several pages of collateral mechanics
before knowing how each quantity enters CVA.

A preferred main-body progression is approximately:

    1. Purpose and CVA object
    2. Transaction setup and structural conditions
    3. Pathwise CVA / event exposure
    4. Pre-default collateral quantity and finite-pool dynamics
    5. Default/restructuring-state bond valuation
    6. Derivative unwind valuation
    7. Worked example / alternative paths to event
    8. Wrong-way risk and MPOR
    9. Implementation
    10. Assumptions, sensitivities, model risk and conclusion

Exact section numbering can differ if a cleaner structure results, but the
CVA/exposure equation should appear before the detailed modeling components.

2. USE B^uw AS THE CENTRAL COLLATERAL QUANTITY
======================================================================

Simplify the current B^def -> B^disp architecture.

Use:

    B_tau^uw

as the central quantity entering exposure.

Define it carefully as:

    B_tau^uw =
        value of the posted bond collateral available to offset the
        derivative claim in the contractual unwind state.

Then:

    E_tau = (V_tau^uw - B_tau^uw)^+.

This gives useful symmetry:

    V_tau^uw = derivative unwind-state value
    B_tau^uw = collateral unwind-state value.

Make clear that B^uw does NOT mean that “the bond itself is unwound.”
It means the value assigned to/available from the posted collateral for
purposes of the derivative unwind.

Do not make B^def and B^disp first-class objects in the core methodology
unless they are genuinely needed.

In particular, the baseline currently has q_disp = 1, so introducing:

    B_tau^def
        ->
    B_tau^disp = q_disp B_tau^def

as two separate central stages adds notation without changing the baseline
economics.

Instead, model B_tau^uw directly.

A general representation can be:

    B_tau^uw
      =
    R_fn(
        N_{tau-}^post,
        P_{tau-},
        bond contractual terms,
        default/restructuring state
    ).

A reduced-form implementation may then use:

    B_tau^uw ≈ R_eff N_{tau-}^post,

subject to the chosen recovery convention and the definition of face/principal.

B^def may still be used locally in the recovery-model discussion if there is
a genuine need to distinguish theoretical event-state value from executable
disposal proceeds, but it should not compete with B^uw in the core framework.

Do NOT rename B^def to B^distress.

“Distress” describes the PRE-default/pre-restructuring credit state. The
important distinction is:

    distress:
        affects P_t and therefore N_t^post before tau;

    default/restructuring:
        determines the event-state collateral value entering B_tau^uw.

3. TREAT DISPOSAL FRICTION AS A REFINEMENT, NOT A CORE STAGE
======================================================================

Do not keep B^disp or q_disp as first-class objects in the baseline if:

    q_disp = 1.

If there is a genuine incremental difference between event-state market value
and the value/proceeds actually available to the dealer because of execution
costs, disposal delay, market impact or transaction-specific liquidation
mechanics, introduce that later as a refinement/sensitivity.

For example, only where justified:

    B_available
        = q_disp B_event-state,

or an equivalent formulation.

Do not visually imply that B_tau^def and an actual disposal transaction are
two sequential physical states both occurring instantaneously at tau.

If disposal occurs after the event and the timing is economically material,
distinguish tau from the disposal/realization time explicitly.

Otherwise absorb the relevant market/disposal economics into B_tau^uw.

Also preserve the important non-double-counting point:

if R_eff or the event-state bond value is already calibrated from observable
restructuring-package market values, CDS auction/defaulted-security prices,
or distressed/default-state market prices, an additional disposal/liquidity
adjustment may double count the same effect.

======================================================================
4. CORRECT THE LANGUAGE AROUND MARKET VALUE
======================================================================

Do NOT say that “market value is no longer relevant” after default.

The correct statement is:

    the PERFORMING-STATE bond market value cannot simply be carried through
    the default/restructuring event as the collateral value.

The bond must instead be valued under an explicit event-state
default/restructuring convention.

This distinction is important because Recovery of Market Value (RMV) is itself
a legitimate recovery convention.

The economic sequence should be clear:

    pre-event P_t
        ->
    determines how much bond is posted;

    default/restructuring event
        ->
    recovery/restructuring model determines B_tau^uw.

For example, under RMV the pre-default market value itself enters the recovery
rule:

    event-state value ~ R x B_{tau-}.

Therefore do not write anything suggesting that market value as a concept is
irrelevant.

5. GIVE LAURENT’S THREE RECOVERY APPROACHES A CLEAR ROLE
======================================================================

In the section modeling B_tau^uw, clearly present all three recovery schemes
discussed by Laurent et al.:

1. Fractional Recovery of Market Value (RMV)

2. Recovery of Treasury (RT)

3. Recovery of Face Value (RFV)

Explain concisely:

- what economic object is recovered under each convention;
- how each convention determines the value of the bond around/default;
- what its implications are for pre-default bond pricing;
- and why these are genuinely different modeling assumptions.

Preserve Laurent’s important principal-versus-coupon result:

under RFV, principal carries the recovery claim whereas unpaid future coupons
do not, so using one conventional risky/Z-spread discount curve indiscriminately
for principal and coupons is generally inconsistent.

However, explicitly separate this from the CVA question.

Laurent’s discounting result concerns PRE-DEFAULT PRICING of a live defaultable
bond.

Our CVA question at tau is:

    what is the value of the posted collateral available in the
    default/restructuring/unwind state?

Do not conflate the two.

6. USE THE GREEK EVIDENCE TO INFORM, NOT DICTATE, THE RECOVERY MODEL
======================================================================

Use the Greek restructuring evidence naturally in support of the recovery
discussion.

The Greek evidence should establish that:

- sovereign credit deterioration can occur over a long period before the
  contractual default/restructuring event;

- bond prices can therefore deteriorate materially before tau;

- the 2012 Greek restructuring was not literally a fixed cash recovery of face;

- old bonds were exchanged for a package of new Greek bonds, EFSF instruments,
  GDP-linked securities and other consideration;

- therefore a practical quantity such as R_eff can be interpreted as the
  MARKET VALUE OF THE RESTRUCTURING/RECOVERY CONSIDERATION PER UNIT OF OLD
  PRINCIPAL, rather than necessarily a literal cash recovery payment.

Thus a reduced-form approximation:

    B_tau^uw ≈ R_eff N_{tau-}^post

can be understood as approximating the richer object:

    B_tau^uw
      =
    MV_tau(
        new bonds
        + cash/cash-like consideration
        + contingent claims
        + other proceeds
    ).

Do NOT claim that Greece establishes RFV as the universally correct sovereign
recovery convention.

Do NOT treat the Greek 21.5 CDS auction Final Price, nominal haircut,
PV/economic haircut, distressed pre-restructuring bond prices, and market value
of restructuring consideration as interchangeable quantities.

Detailed empirical support belongs in the appendix; the main methodology
should contain only the conclusions needed to motivate the modeling choice.

8. BRING THE FINITE COLLATERAL-POOL CAP INTO THE FIRST N^post PRESENTATION
======================================================================

The finite collateral pool is central to the economics and should not appear
only as a later qualification.

Establish from the outset:

    N_t^post
      =
    min(N_t^req, N^pool),

with approximately:

    N_t^req
      =
    V_t / (q_CSA P_t),

subject to the exact contractual mechanics.

Therefore:

    B_t^post
      =
    P_t N_t^post
      =
    min(
        V_t/q_CSA,
        P_t N^pool
    ).

This immediately gives two regimes.

REGIME 1 — CAP NOT BINDING

    N_t^post < N^pool.

As sovereign credit deteriorates:

    P_t falls
        ->
    N_t^req rises
        ->
    more bond principal is called/posted.

The increased quantity partially offsets the declining value per unit.

REGIME 2 — CAP BINDING

    N_t^post = N^pool.

Once the pool is exhausted, no additional collateral can be posted.

Further deterioration in P_t therefore directly reduces collateral coverage.

This is the central reason the amount of collateral available at tau depends
on the PRE-DEFAULT credit path.

Do not first state:

    B_t^post ≈ V_t/q_CSA

as though sufficient collateral is always available and introduce the cap only
later.

The cap should be present in the first mathematical statement of the
collateral-posting mechanism.

9. KEEP VM TERMINOLOGY PRECISE
======================================================================

Do not describe VM itself as “the derivative.”

Let:

    V_t

denote the dealer-side derivative MtM/value.

Under the simplified CSA mechanics this determines the cash-equivalent VM
requirement.

Variation margin is the collateral called/transferred as a consequence of the
derivative valuation under the CSA.

A concise formulation is sufficient, for example:

    “Let V_t > 0 denote the dealer-side derivative MtM, which determines the
     cash-equivalent VM requirement under the simplified CSA mechanics
     considered here.”

Do not over-explain this point.

10. CLARIFY THE TWO PATHS TO THE EVENT
======================================================================

Retain the useful distinction between:

PATH I:
    gradual sovereign deterioration causes the collateral pool cap to become
    binding before the loss-trigger event;

PATH II:
    a sufficiently discontinuous jump/default/restructuring occurs while
    unused collateral remains in the pool.

But state the modeling conclusion explicitly.

These are NOT two competing methodologies that are arbitrarily chosen.

Nor should unsupported probabilities be assigned to them.

In a full stochastic methodology, they are endogenous outcomes of the SAME
simulated process:

    sovereign credit path
        ->
    P_t
        ->
    N_t^post
        ->
    tau
        ->
    B_tau^uw
        ->
    E_tau.

Some simulated paths deteriorate enough before tau that:

    N_{tau-}^post = N^pool.

Others reach the event while:

    N_{tau-}^post < N^pool.

Their relative frequency/probability should therefore emerge from the modeled
credit/bond-price/default dynamics.

For a simplified first implementation or worked example, it is acceptable to
show:

    - cap-bound scenario;
    - jump-from-current-posting scenario;

as scenario/bounding calculations.

If this is what the current implementation does, state it explicitly.

Do NOT average the two using unsupported scenario probabilities.

Do NOT imply that the event at tau itself causes the unused collateral pool to
become posted.

If the full N^pool enters a default-state scenario, it must be because either:

    (a) the cap became binding BEFORE tau; or
    (b) transaction documents establish that the entire pool is legally
        available to satisfy the dealer claim irrespective of CSA VM calls.

14. KEEP WRONG-WAY RISK, BUT EXPRESS IT THROUGH THE CORE MODEL
======================================================================

Retain the distinction between:

PRE-DEFAULT DEPENDENCE:

    sovereign deterioration
        ->
    P_t falls
        ->
    N_t^post changes
        ->
    collateral coverage changes;

and:

EVENT-STATE STRUCTURAL DEPENDENCE:

    the same sovereign default/restructuring event
        ->
    triggers derivative unwind
        AND
    impairs the collateral.

The latter is structural rather than merely a statistical correlation between
two independent events.

However, do not imply that this eliminates stochastic modeling.

The joint behavior of:

    P_t,
    V_t,
    N_t^post,
    cap-exhaustion timing,
    tau,
    V_tau^uw,
    B_tau^uw

remains stochastic.

15. KEEP MPOR AS AN IMPLEMENTATION REFINEMENT
======================================================================

Retain the important security-collateral point:

    quantity freezes; value does not.

At the last collateral call before default/unwind, the bond quantity posted is
fixed over the MPOR, while its market value can continue to evolve.

This differs from treating collateral as a frozen cash amount.

However, MPOR should not interrupt the main derivation.

Present it after the central CVA, collateral-quantity, recovery and unwind
framework has been established.

Keep any finding about the existing implementation freezing collateral value
tagged as [IMPL] pending confirmation.

In particular verify:

TIMING

    t < tau       pre-default/distress state
    tau-          collateral quantity immediately before event
    tau           loss-trigger / derivative unwind state
    tau+epsilon   only if a separate disposal process is explicitly modeled

NOTATION

    V_t               derivative MtM
    N^pool            available collateral pool principal/notional
    N_t^req           CSA-required principal/notional
    N_t^post          actually posted principal/notional
    P_t               pre-default bond price
    V_tau^uw          derivative unwind value
    B_tau^uw          collateral value available against unwind claim
    R_eff             effective event-state/restructuring value per unit face
    q_CSA             contractual CSA valuation percentage
    LGD               residual counterparty loss severity

Avoid unnecessary intermediate symbols.

CORE MODEL

    N_t^post
      =
    min(
        V_t/(q_CSA P_t),
        N^pool
    )

    B_tau^uw
      =
    recovery/restructuring valuation of N_{tau-}^post

and, under the reduced-form baseline,

    B_tau^uw
      ≈
    R_eff N_{tau-}^post

followed by

    E_tau
      =
    (V_tau^uw - B_tau^uw)^+

and

    CVA_0
      =
    LGD sum_i DF(0,t_i) E[E_ti] Delta S_i.

The expectation must remain pathwise inside the positive part.

Delta S_i is the marginal probability of the MODELED LOSS-TRIGGER EVENT, not
casually the collateral issuer's probability or derivative counterparty's
default probability without reference to C2.

22. TARGET QUALITY OF THE FINAL REVISION
======================================================================

The final paper should feel like ONE MODEL progressively decomposed:

    What is CVA?
        ->
    What is exposure at the relevant event?
        ->
    What are the derivative and collateral worth at that event?
        ->
    How much collateral got there?
        ->
    How do we model its restructuring/default value?
        ->
    How do we integrate this pathwise over default probability?
        ->
    How do we implement it in the existing engine?

A reader should not need to remember several pages of transaction mechanics
before discovering how they enter CVA.

Preserve the technically valuable material already developed, but remove
notation and stages that do not materially contribute to the baseline model.

In particular, the preferred conceptual backbone is:

    sovereign credit evolution
        ->
    P_t and V_t
        ->
    N_t^post = min(N_t^req, N^pool)
        ->
    loss-trigger event tau
        ->
    V_tau^uw and B_tau^uw
        ->
    E_tau = (V_tau^uw - B_tau^uw)^+
        ->
    CVA.

Use this backbone consistently in the introduction, methodology, worked
example, implementation mapping and conclusion.
