## Response to G1 – Target Audience & Deployment Context

The target for V3 is best viewed as a **pre-production quantitative research library**, positioned between a research prototype and an enterprise production system.

It is therefore closest to **Option B**, with one important emphasis:

> Prioritize improvements that increase **quantitative realism and reproducibility** over enterprise deployment infrastructure.

The intended audience is:

* quantitative researchers,
* quantitative developers,
* model validation teams,
* corporate treasury modelling teams,

rather than production support or operations teams.

Accordingly, V3 should focus on making the library a robust, explainable, and reproducible modelling framework that is suitable for model review and future productionization.

### Priorities for V3

Examples include:

* richer model realism,
* calibration framework,
* improved numerical methods,
* deterministic reproducibility,
* configuration serialization,
* richer diagnostics,
* validation reports,
* result provenance,
* explainable decomposition of pricing results,
* benchmark comparisons,
* parameter sensitivity analysis.

These improvements directly strengthen the quantitative model while also making it easier to validate.

### Enterprise Features

Items such as:

* market data adapters,
* scheduling,
* deployment,
* monitoring,
* entitlement,
* operational alerting,
* distributed execution,
* production APIs,
* enterprise authentication,

should primarily be treated as **future roadmap items**.

They should be assessed and documented within the production gap analysis, but should generally **not** be implemented during V3 unless they naturally support reproducibility or model validation.

### Guiding Principle

If there is a trade-off between:

* improving quantitative realism and model quality, or
* implementing production infrastructure,

prefer the former.

The objective of V3 is to produce a library that a quantitative researcher or model validation team would regard as technically strong and well engineered.

Enterprise deployment should remain a separate and subsequent evolution once the quantitative model itself has matured.

## Response to Remaining Gray Areas (G2–G6)

### G2 – Calibration Scope

#### G2a – Historical Data vs Framework

Prefer **framework-first**.

V3 should build a complete calibration framework capable of accepting historical or market data, but it should use synthetic or illustrative datasets unless suitable production-quality datasets are already available.

The objective is to demonstrate a sound calibration workflow rather than create a production market-data pipeline.

Design the interfaces so that real CDS spreads, LP spread histories, macro time series, or other market data can be plugged in later with minimal changes.

---

#### G2b – Calibration Method

Support both calibration paradigms conceptually, but prioritize one.

Priority:

1. Cross-sectional market calibration (e.g. fitting CIR hazard parameters to an observed CDS or hazard term structure)
2. Historical estimation (MLE, moment matching, etc.)

Rationale:

The pricing library ultimately prices today's facility using today's market information. Cross-sectional calibration therefore aligns most naturally with the intended use of the framework.

Historical estimation should be supported through the same calibration architecture but can initially be demonstrated with synthetic data.

---

### G3 – Portfolio vs Single Name

V3 should remain a **single-name framework**.

The objective is to deepen model quality rather than broaden scope.

Portfolio simulation introduces a fundamentally different layer:

* borrower heterogeneity
* portfolio dependence
* common factors
* concentration risk
* aggregation
* capital allocation

Those belong naturally in a future V4.

A well-designed single-name architecture should make later portfolio expansion straightforward.

---

### G4 – Performance Targets

Current performance is sufficient for the intended research-library use case.

Do not optimize prematurely.

Instead:

* profile the current implementation;
* identify computational bottlenecks;
* document where acceleration would help.

Only implement performance improvements that are low-risk and architecture-preserving.

Examples:

* improved vectorization;
* avoiding unnecessary allocations;
* optional NumPy optimizations.

Do **not** introduce Numba, GPU implementations, multiprocessing, or other advanced optimization frameworks unless profiling demonstrates that they are necessary.

Performance engineering can become a dedicated future phase after the quantitative model has stabilized.

---

### G5 – Stochastic Interest Rates

For V3, deterministic term-structure-aware discounting is sufficient.

Priority:

* support deterministic OIS / SOFR zero curves;
* support arbitrary deterministic discount curves;
* preserve existing flat-rate capability.

Do **not** introduce stochastic short-rate models (Hull–White, Vasicek, CIR rates, etc.) during V3.

Reasoning:

The dominant uncertainty in the current framework remains

* credit quality,
* utilisation,
* liquidity premium,
* macro dynamics.

Adding stochastic rates substantially increases dimensionality while contributing comparatively little to the current modelling objective.

Treat stochastic interest rates as a natural future V4 enhancement.

---

### G6 – V3 Stage Granularity

Five stages is an appropriate level of granularity.

It provides enough separation for clear implementation and review without fragmenting the work excessively.

A suggested structure is:

**V3.1 — Calibration Framework**

* calibration architecture
* parameter estimation
* synthetic examples

---

**V3.2 — Quantitative Realism**

* deterministic curve support
* richer recovery assumptions
* improved numerical modelling

---

**V3.3 — Validation & Diagnostics**

* benchmark reports
* sensitivity analysis
* convergence studies
* calibration diagnostics

---

**V3.4 — Engineering Hardening**

* configuration serialization
* reproducibility
* reporting
* improved APIs
* documentation

---

**V3.5 — Production Gap Assessment**

* summarize remaining gaps;
* identify what belongs in V4;
* document enterprise roadmap.

The intent is that each stage produces a coherent, reviewable improvement while preserving the project's incremental development philosophy.

---

## Overall Guiding Principle

When making implementation decisions throughout V3, prefer improvements that increase:

* quantitative realism;
* calibration capability;
* numerical robustness;
* explainability;
* reproducibility;
* validation quality.

Avoid adding complexity solely for completeness or academic sophistication.

The goal is to produce a library that a quantitative researcher, model validator, or quantitative developer would regard as technically strong, well organized, and ready for future productionization—not to build a complete production platform within V3.
