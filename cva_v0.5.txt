2. REFRAME THE STRUCTURAL CONDITIONS C1–C3
============================================================

Retain C1 and C2 conceptually:

C1. VM is a security rather than cash, so its market value is stochastic and can be strongly dependent on issuer credit quality.

C2. Under the assumed transaction mechanics, default/restructuring of the collateral issuer/bond is the economically relevant loss-trigger event because it triggers termination/unwind of the Repack swap.

Make clear that C2 is transaction-specific and must be verified against the relevant transaction documents. Do not write “SPV default = bond issuer default” or “SPV credit curve = issuer credit curve.”

Rewrite C3.

Do NOT simply state that the €51m bond pool is structurally overcollateralized relative to the derivative and therefore the entire €51m automatically protects the dealer.

Instead explain the collateral dynamics.

At each pre-default time:

    B_t^posted
      = min(V_t / q_CSA, B_t^cap)

and equivalently, for a single bond collateral pool,

    N_t^posted
      = min(N_t^required, N_pool)

where approximately

    N_t^required = V_t / (q_CSA P_t)

subject to the exact contractual mechanics.

As the sovereign/bond deteriorates:

    P_t ↓
      => N_t^required ↑
      => progressively more bond notional may be called as VM.

Eventually the available collateral cap may bind:

    N_t^posted = N_pool.

This is an important Repack-specific mechanism.

The current example has approximately:

    V_0 = €25.16m
    q_CSA = 85%
    B_0^pool = €44.41m
    N_pool = €51m

so current required bond market value is approximately:

    V_0 / q_CSA = €29.60m,

which is less than the full pool market value.

Therefore the full €51m is NOT automatically the amount posted today.

However, in a gradual sovereign deterioration scenario, falling bond prices can cause progressively more of the pool to be posted, potentially reaching:

    N_tau-^posted = N_pool = €51m

before the contractual default/restructuring event.

Use the Greek sovereign crisis as empirical motivation for why gradual deterioration before restructuring is economically plausible: severe sovereign distress and bond-price deterioration can precede the contractual restructuring/default event for a substantial period.

Also acknowledge jump-to-default risk. A sufficiently discontinuous default can occur before the collateral cap is reached, in which case:

    N_tau-^posted < N_pool.

Therefore €51m should enter the worked default example only as an explicit “collateral cap binding immediately prior to default” assumption, unless the transaction documents establish that the entire collateral pool is legally pledged/available to the dealer irrespective of CSA VM calls.

Distinguish carefully between:
(a) the total SPV asset/collateral pool,
(b) collateral actually posted under the CSA, and
(c) assets legally available to satisfy the dealer close-out claim.

3. REWRITE SECTION 2.1 AROUND PRE-DEFAULT VS DEFAULT-STATE COLLATERAL
============================================================

The key point of §2.1 should NOT be merely:

    B_tau- > V_tau-

because of “structural overcollateralization.”

Instead explain:

VM mechanics are intended to keep the performing exposure collateralized. With q_CSA < 1,

    B_t^posted ≈ V_t / q_CSA > V_t

while sufficient collateral remains available.

Therefore a naïve calculation such as

    (V_tau- - B_tau-^posted)^+

will often be approximately zero.

But this does NOT establish that default-state exposure is zero.

The economically relevant exposure is:

    E_tau
      = (V_tau^co - B_tau^real)^+.

The central modeling problem is therefore the transition:

    pre-default collateral state
        ->
    default/restructuring collateral value
        ->
    realizable collateral value.

Make the distinction between tau- and tau visually and conceptually clear.

4. PRESENT THE CORE CVA ARCHITECTURE IN THREE STAGES
============================================================

Organize the collateral methodology around:

    credit-state-dependent pre-default bond MTM
        ->
    explicit contractual unwind/default trigger
        ->
    post-default/restructuring liquidation value.

More operationally:

    P_t
      ->
    N_tau-^posted
      ->
    B_tau^def
      ->
    B_tau^real.

Stage 1 — Pre-default collateral dynamics / CSA mechanics

Model the bond's market value as sovereign credit quality deteriorates and determine how much bond notional has actually been posted immediately before the event.

Do not assume the bond remains near par until default.

Stage 2 — Default/restructuring-state bond valuation

Define generally:

    B_tau^def
      = R_function(
            N_tau-^posted,
            P_tau-,
            bond contractual terms,
            sovereign/default state,
            recovery/restructuring assumptions
        ).

Do NOT make RFV part of the fundamental definition of the CVA methodology.

RFV should be presented as one tractable implementation of this more general object.

Stage 3 — Realization

If there are genuine incremental liquidation/disposal effects not already captured in the default-state market value:

    B_tau^real = q_liq B_tau^def.

However, if B_tau^def is calibrated from CDS auction prices, distressed/defaulted bond market prices, or market values of restructuring consideration that already incorporate realization/liquidity effects, baseline q_liq should normally be 1 to avoid double counting.

5. DEFAULT/RESTRUCTURING BOND VALUATION: KEEP MAIN BODY SIMPLE
============================================================

In the main methodology, state that several recovery/default-value conventions are possible and that the selected convention is a modeling/calibration choice.

For the practical baseline, consider the reduced-form approximation:

    B_tau^def ≈ R_eff N_tau-^posted,

where R_eff is NOT necessarily interpreted as a literal cash recovery payment.

Define R_eff more generally as:

    effective market value of the recovery/restructuring consideration
    per unit of old bond face/notional at the relevant unwind/liquidation time.

Thus economically:

    B_tau^def
      = MV_tau(
            new bonds
            + cash/cash-like consideration
            + contingent claims
            + other restructuring proceeds
        )

may be approximated by:

    R_eff N_tau-^posted.

This gives the model a tractable RFV-like implementation without claiming that actual sovereign restructurings literally pay a fixed percentage of face in cash.

Do not state that empirical evidence proves RFV is universally correct.

6. PUT THE DETAILED RECOVERY LITERATURE IN AN APPENDIX
============================================================

Add an appendix reviewing only the recovery literature relevant to the modeling choice.

Use Laurent et al., “Sovereign Recovery Schemes: Discounting and Risk Management Issues.”

Accurately describe ALL THREE recovery schemes analyzed by Laurent:

A. Fractional Recovery of Market Value (RMV)

Recovery at default is proportional to immediately pre-default market value:

    recovery ~ R × B_tau-.

Explain its pricing implications and why it is mathematically convenient.

Explain why it is not necessarily the most natural empirical description of a sovereign restructuring in which old claims are exchanged for a package whose value is substantially related to the old claim's principal rather than simply a fraction of its last traded price.

B. Fractional Recovery of Treasury / cash-flow recovery (RT)

Recovery is linked to a proportional recovery of the remaining contractual/default-free cash-flow value.

Explain the corresponding pricing interpretation and why it resembles proportional recovery of the remaining promised claim more than an exchange into a materially different restructuring package.

C. Fractional Recovery of Face Value / Par (RFV/FRP)

Recovery attaches to principal/face value while unpaid future coupons do not receive the same recovery claim.

Explain Laurent's important result that this creates different effective risky discounting for principal and coupons.

Under RFV/FRP, a single conventional risky/Z-spread curve applied indiscriminately to principal and coupon cashflows is generally inconsistent with the recovery economics.

Be precise: this does NOT mean that two independently calibrated sovereign default/hazard processes are required.

A common hazard/survival process combined with different recovery treatment for principal and coupon can generate the required economics.

Also make clear that Laurent's principal-versus-coupon discounting result concerns PRE-DEFAULT pricing of a defaultable bond. It must not be confused with the separate CVA question of determining the market/realization value of collateral at the unwind/default event.

State clearly what Laurent actually concludes and distinguish the paper's theoretical/modeling conclusions from our own choice of approximation.

Do not attribute conclusions from the Greek empirical paper to Laurent or vice versa.

12. WRONG-WAY RISK
============================================================

Retain the concept of structural wrong-way risk but sharpen it.

There are two related mechanisms:

A. Pre-default collateral deterioration:

    sovereign credit deterioration
      -> bond price falls
      -> more bond quantity is required under CSA
      -> N_t^posted rises
      -> eventually collateral cap may bind.

This collateral-call mechanism partially mitigates declining bond value until the pool is exhausted.

B. Default-state dependence:

If the same issuer-default/restructuring event both triggers swap unwind and impairs the collateral bond, the loss-trigger event and collateral impairment are structurally linked.

This is not merely a statistical correlation assumption.

However, do not claim that this eliminates all stochastic dependence/modeling issues. V_tau^co, P_tau-, timing of cap exhaustion, jump-to-default behavior and residual counterparty exposure may still be jointly stochastic.

19. FINAL METHODOLOGY MESSAGE
============================================================

The paper should ultimately communicate the following logic cleanly:

The CVA problem is not solved by applying a static haircut to the performing market value of the sovereign bond.

Instead:

    sovereign credit state
        ->
    pre-default bond MTM
        ->
    CSA determines quantity of bond posted
        ->
    contractual loss-trigger event occurs
        ->
    derivative is revalued to V_tau^co
        ->
    posted bond is valued under an explicit
    default/restructuring recovery model
        ->
    any genuinely separate realization effect is applied
        ->
    residual positive exposure generates CVA.

The compact mathematical representation is:

    N_t^posted
      = min(N_t^required, N_pool)

    B_tau^def
      = R_function(N_tau-^posted,
                   P_tau-,
                   bond terms,
                   default/restructuring state)

    B_tau^real
      = q_liq B_tau^def

    E_tau
      = (V_tau^co - B_tau^real)^+

    CVA_0
      = LGD Σ_i DF(0,t_i)
          E[E_ti] ΔS_i.

For the simple baseline implementation:

    B_tau^def ≈ R_eff N_tau-^posted,

but explicitly identify this as a reduced-form approximation to the market value of potentially much richer restructuring proceeds, not a universal law of sovereign recovery.

The result should be concise enough to function as a methodology white paper, while the appendices contain the literature evidence, Greek case study, implementation investigation, regulatory discussion, and transaction-specific documentary support.
